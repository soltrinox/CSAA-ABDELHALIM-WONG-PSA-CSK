# Contra Costa C26-02801: the core wrong and the *Moradi-Shalal* preservation clause

> **Public commentary published September 22, 2026.** This page is not a court filing, is not service of any paper, and does not restart any Code of Civil Procedure section 128.7 period. The legal formulation below was sent by email to CSAA and its counsel on September 22, 2026. The verified complaint is on file in Contra Costa Superior Court as Case No. C26-02801.

## Banner argument

**The core wrong is independent common-law deceit about completed historical acts.**

CSAA wrote that it had "concluded our investigation" and had "carefully review[ed] the facts and circumstances" of the claim. The complaint alleges that CSAA possessed a police report identifying the 911, body-worn-camera, and computer-aided-dispatch materials, but its claim file recorded no request, receipt, or review of those materials before closure. The action does not seek private enforcement of Insurance Code section 790.03, policy benefits, or the value of the underlying bodily-injury claim. It seeks reliance damages under Civil Code sections 1709, 1710, subdivision (1), and 3333 for an affirmative representation about investigative work CSAA claimed it had already completed.

[*Moradi-Shalal v. Fireman's Fund Insurance Companies*](../_shared/moradi-shalal-carveout-apr27/README.md) (1988) 46 Cal.3d 287, 304-305 expressly preserves appropriate traditional common-law actions against insurers, including fraud, while barring an implied private cause of action under Insurance Code section 790.03.

## The preservation clause

*Moradi-Shalal* holds that Insurance Code section 790.03 does not create an implied private cause of action for a third-party claimant. At pages 304-305, the California Supreme Court separately states that, apart from administrative remedies, courts retain jurisdiction to impose civil damages or other remedies against insurers in appropriate common-law actions based on traditional theories including fraud.

C26-02801 invokes the preserved common-law channel. The [verified complaint](VERIFIED-COMPLAINT-C26-02801.pdf) pleads one cause of action for intentional deceit under Civil Code sections 1709 and 1710, subdivision (1). It does not plead a direct cause of action under section 790.03, third-party insurance bad faith, breach of an insurance contract, or entitlement to policy proceeds.

The distinction is between:

- a claim that an insurer violated statutory settlement duties, which *Moradi-Shalal* bars a private third-party claimant from enforcing directly; and
- a claim that a commercial speaker knowingly made a false factual representation intending that its direct recipient rely, which Civil Code sections 1709 and 1710 independently prohibit.

## The representation concerns completed historical acts

The February 25, 2021 [denial letter](../MERITS-EXHIBITS/CSAA_Denial_Letter-Feb-25-2021.pdf) states:

> "We have concluded our investigation of your claim."

It also states that CSAA reached its decision "after carefully reviewing the facts and circumstances of your claim."

The actionable representation pleaded in C26-02801 is not the legal conclusion that the insured was not liable. It concerns what CSAA claimed it had already done: completed an investigation and carefully reviewed the relevant facts. Whether CSAA requested, received, reviewed, and documented particular evidence is a historical file-state question.

## The alleged falsity

The complaint alleges that CSAA obtained the police report concerning the collision. That report identified the 911 recording, body-worn-camera evidence, computer-aided-dispatch records, and associated reference information. Those materials concerned contact, eyewitness observations, and the insured's statements.

The complaint further alleges that the claim file recorded:

- no request for those report-identified materials;
- no receipt of those materials;
- no review of those materials; and
- closure twenty-one days after the collision.

Obtaining a police report or receiving the insured's account may constitute isolated claim-processing acts. The pleaded factual question is whether those acts truthfully support CSAA's representation that the investigation had been concluded after careful review when the report itself identified material sources that were not requested or reviewed.

## The independent common-law duty

Civil Code section 1709 imposes liability when one person willfully deceives another with intent to induce that person to alter a position to injury or risk. Civil Code section 1710, subdivision (1), includes the suggestion as a fact of that which is not true by one who does not believe it to be true.

The complaint does not derive its private cause of action from section 790.03. It alleges that CSAA voluntarily made a positive factual representation directly to Plaintiff. The generally applicable common-law duty is the duty not to make that representation with knowledge of falsity and intent to induce reliance.

Title 10, California Code of Regulations, sections 2695.3 and 2695.7 remain relevant regulatory context. They describe claim-file documentation and the thorough, fair, and objective investigation required of insurers. They do not supply the private damages cause of action pleaded in C26-02801.

## Knowledge, intent, reliance, and injury

The complaint alleges that CSAA exclusively controlled the claim file, activity history, request records, production indices, and audit trails. CSAA therefore knew what its personnel had and had not requested or reviewed when it described the investigation as concluded.

It further alleges that Plaintiff had no access to that proprietary system and relied on the direct representation of completed investigative work. The alleged alteration of position was forbearance from immediately obtaining the identified public-safety records during the ordinary retention period. The pleaded injury is the incremental out-of-pocket cost of delayed retrieval, restoration, authentication, and reconstruction, according to proof.

The requested damages are not policy proceeds, collision damages, or the value of a refused settlement. They are alleged reliance damages caused by the affirmative deceit.

## Later filings and the verdict

CSAA's April 9, 2026 anti-SLAPP memorandum states that CSAA ["reviewed the available evidence, including the statements of its Insured"](../DEFENSE-FILINGS/CGC-25-631802/2026-04-09__mpa__mpa-anti-slapp-mpas---anti-slapp/extraction.md) and characterized the denial as statutory compliance.

Its April 22, 2026 section 436 memorandum states that ["CSAA performed an investigation, determined that its insured was not liable, and informed Plaintiff of their decision"](../03-CASE-CGC-25-631802/motion-strike-apr2026/PARSED/TEXT/MPA2-CCP436.md), followed by an argument that the jury agreed with CSAA's denial position.

The personal-injury verdict decided the insured's negligence on the evidence and instructions presented at the 2025 trial. It did not decide what CSAA's claim file contained on February 25, 2021, whether CSAA requested the report-identified recordings, whether its completed-investigation representation was true, whether Plaintiff relied, or whether that reliance caused independent retrieval expenses.

The verdict therefore does not adjudicate the investigation or the truth of the denial letter. Later litigation statements are linked here as filed party positions relevant to the allegations of knowledge, adoption, and ratification. They are not pleaded in C26-02801 as separate liability-producing acts.
