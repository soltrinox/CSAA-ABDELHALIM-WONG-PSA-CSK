This page collects the conclusion, the inference, and the consequence already drawn in papers a court has seen. Each block has the same three lines.

**Conclusion.** What the record shows.

**Inference.** What the filed paper asks the reader to draw from that record.

**Consequence.** The legal result that paper assigns to the choice.

A consequence is not a criminal verdict. Where a statute uses the word "guilty," this page says so and keeps that word inside the civil standard.

## The statutory word "guilty"

Civil Code section 3294 is the civil punitive-damages statute. The defense motion papers in CGC-25-631802 put that statute in issue and quote the statutory phrase "guilty of oppression, fraud, or malice." That phrase is a clear-and-convincing civil standard for punitive damages. It is not a criminal conviction, and this site does not enter one.

The same defense papers answer a complaint that describes an institutional deny-delay-defend pattern: a denial that represents an investigation as complete, then later papers that treat the same denial as protected litigation conduct. The inference those pleadings put before a court is institutional purpose. The consequence they assign is exposure under the causes of action and the punitive statute already pleaded, not a finding that anyone has been convicted.

## 1. CSAA certified an investigation the claim file does not document

**Conclusion.** On February 25, 2021, CSAA wrote that it had concluded its investigation and that the insured was not liable. The filed Contra Costa complaint alleges that the claim file recorded no request, receipt, or review of the police-report-identified 911, body-worn-camera, and dispatch materials before that closure.

**Inference.** A letter that certifies a completed investigation, sent about a file that does not document those steps, is a representation about completed historical acts.

**Consequence.** C26-02801 seeks reliance damages for common-law deceit under Civil Code sections 1709, 1710, subdivision (1), and 3333. CGC-25-631802 pleads the same letter as a negligent misrepresentation and uses Insurance Code section 790.03 and 10 CCR section 2695.7 as the standard of care, not as a private right of action under section 790.03. *Moradi-Shalal v. Fireman's Fund Insurance Companies* (1988) 46 Cal.3d 287, 304-305 preserves traditional fraud actions while barring an implied private action under section 790.03.

## 2. The carrier then called that letter litigation

**Conclusion.** On April 9, 2026, CSAA filed special motions to strike in CGC-25-631801 and CGC-25-631802. Winning that vehicle required treating the February 25, 2021 denial as protected petitioning in anticipation of litigation, including under Civil Code section 47, subdivision (b).

**Inference.** An insurer that tells a court the claim denial was undertaken for litigation has described an adversarial purpose during the claim window. A thorough, fair, and objective investigation under 10 CCR section 2695.7, subdivision (d), is a different purpose. The filed analysis treats those two descriptions as incompatible.

**Consequence.** Four results already stated on the homepage follow from that choice.

1. The purpose the regulation forbids is conceded. Saying the denial was adversarial is the concealment claim, not a defense to it.
2. The privilege is overclaimed. The letter predates the litigation it is said to anticipate.
3. The section 425.17, subdivision (c), commercial-speech carve-out is in issue before either anti-SLAPP prong, under *Simpson Strong-Tie Co. v. Gore* (2010) 49 Cal.4th 12.
4. An order granting a special motion to strike is immediately appealable under Code of Civil Procedure section 904.1, subdivision (a)(13). The July 1 and August 6, 2026 orders are the orders that opened that appellate lane.

## 3. The trial court forbade the jury to use statutes the jury had already encountered

**Conclusion.** On April 23, 2025, the deliberating jury asked whether it should consider Vehicle Code sections 22107 and 21804, subdivision (a). The trial court, Judge Garrett L. Wong, answered in writing: "You may not consider VC Sections 22107 and or [sic] 21804 subdivision A, nor will the Court instruct the Jury on those provisions." Orally, the court said neither section was part of the evidence: "They simply are not permitted to consider that as evidence in the case, period." (RT 11, p. 7, ll. 23-27; p. 8, ll. 4-9.) Appellant's Opening Brief states that the defense marked the complaint as Defense Exhibit 500, that the complaint pleaded those Vehicle Code sections, and that the jury could see those sections in that exhibit. On that account, the "not in evidence" premise was factually inaccurate as well as legally wrong.

**Inference.** The jury asked for a legal standard it had found in the evidence the defense put before it. The court answered by deciding that the jury could not use that standard and would not be instructed on it. Evidence Code section 312 assigns the jury the application of law to fact. Evidence Code sections 310 and 451 assign the court the declaration of statutory law. The filed reply states the category error in one sentence: statutes are law the court declares, not evidence that must be admitted.

**Consequence.** The opening brief and the August 20, 2026 reply assign that dual prohibition as a violation of Code of Civil Procedure section 614. Section 614 requires that when a deliberating jury desires to be informed on a point of law, the information required must be given. The opening brief states that a deliberation-stage refusal of this kind goes "to the substance of the right of trial by jury" and is deemed prejudicial absent very exceptional circumstances, citing *Nelson v. Southern Pacific Co.* (1937) 8 Cal.2d 648, *Halada v. Venice Lake Park, Inc.* (1955) 132 Cal.App.2d 788, and *Smith v. Shankman* (1962) 208 Cal.App.2d 177. The tables of authorities in the opening brief and the reply list California Constitution article I, section 7, article I, section 16, and the Fourteenth Amendment on the pages that state that violation (reply brief pages 15 and 24). Article VI, section 13 is the miscarriage standard the reply applies to prejudice. The verdict was 9 to 3, the bare civil majority.

The reply also answers the waiver and invited-error opposition. It states that the dual prohibition was formulated and delivered before the colloquy later characterized as an invitation, and that the object of the on-record discussion was unsupervised juror consultation of uninstructed text, not a rule that the statutes could never be declared. (RT 11, p. 7, ll. 19-27; p. 5, ll. 15-25.)
