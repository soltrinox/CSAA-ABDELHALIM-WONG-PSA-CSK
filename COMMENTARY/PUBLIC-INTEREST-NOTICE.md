## 1. Public court proceedings and public records

The collision was on a public street, at Tehama and 5th Street in San Francisco, on February 4, 2021. The trial of that collision, the reporter's transcripts, and the later superior-court files are public court proceedings.

On September 22, 2026, the plaintiff sent counsel a statement that public records documenting corporate conduct may be read, discussed, and published, and that publication of those records is not misconduct because a corporation or its attorneys would prefer fewer people to know. ## 2. A licensed insurer's written certification

On February 25, 2021, CSAA Insurance Exchange sent a denial letter stating that it had concluded its investigation. That letter is an ordinary-course claims communication from a licensed insurer. It is already an exhibit on this site: [February 25, 2021 CSAA denial letter](../MERITS-EXHIBITS/CSAA_Denial_Letter-Feb-25-2021.pdf).

The Third Amended Complaint in CGC-25-631802 treats that writing as the representational wrong: pre-litigation claim-handling, not petitioning. Insurance Code section 790.03 and 10 CCR section 2695.7 are pleaded as the standard of care, not as a private cause of action under section 790.03. The same complaint pleads a reasonable-consumer test under Business and Professions Code section 17200. Element citations: [MERITS.md](../MERITS.md).

The verified complaint in Contra Costa Case No. C26-02801 pleads common-law deceit about completed investigative acts, and invokes the preservation of traditional fraud actions in *Moradi-Shalal v. Fireman's Fund Insurance Companies* (1988) 46 Cal.3d 287, 304-305. The exact clerk filing date and a conformed-stamp image are not present in the local copy. Service of summons and complaint was not perfected as of September 22, 2026. [C26-02801](../04-CASE-C26-02801/INDEX.md).

Court proceedings are public. Insurance claim-handling is a regulated industry. Both points are already stated on the homepage under [Speech, petition, and why this archive is public](../README.md#speech-petition-and-why-this-archive-is-public).

## 5. Speech and petition are different rights

The First Amendment protects speech and the right to petition. Article I, section 2 of the California Constitution protects speech. Article I, sections 3 and 16 protect petition and jury trial. Article I, section 7 and the Fourteenth Amendment protect due process, including access to the courts. Those rights are stated on the homepage and in the July 27, 2026 letter. This page is speech. The complaints, oppositions, and notices of appeal are the petitions.
