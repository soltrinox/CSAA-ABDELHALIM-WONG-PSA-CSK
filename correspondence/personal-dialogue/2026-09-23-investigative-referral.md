The present lawsuit does not seek insurance-policy benefits or attempt to create a private cause of action under Insurance Code section 790.03. The claim concerns an alleged affirmative misrepresentation about historical acts, specifically whether CSAA truthfully represented that investigative work had already been completed.

California Civil Code sections 1709 and 1710 address liability for deceit and false representations of fact, while Civil Code section 3333 addresses tort damages. The California Supreme Court held in Moradi-Shalal v. Fireman's Fund Ins. Cos. (1988) 46 Cal.3d 287 that third-party claimants may not privately enforce Insurance Code section 790.03, while recognizing the continued availability of otherwise existing common-law causes of action. The complaint proceeds on that distinction.

The Fair Claims Settlement Practices Regulations also provide relevant regulatory context. Title 10, California Code of Regulations, section 2695.7 addresses investigation and written factual bases for claim denials. Those regulations are not asserted here as an independent private cause of action. They provide context for evaluating what an insurer represents when it states that an investigation has been completed.

The damages alleged in this action are likewise narrow. After being told that CSAA had completed its investigation, I did not have access to CSAA's internal claim database and had no way of knowing that report-identified evidence allegedly had not been obtained. I later incurred expenses retrieving, restoring, authenticating, and reconstructing public-safety records that could have been obtained closer to the time of the collision. Those reconstruction expenses, according to proof, are the damages sought in the present action.

CSAA and its counsel are also presently pursuing vexatious-litigant relief against me. Because those proceedings concern my continued access to the courts, I believe there is legitimate public interest in examining both the underlying evidence and the positions being advanced in the litigation.
