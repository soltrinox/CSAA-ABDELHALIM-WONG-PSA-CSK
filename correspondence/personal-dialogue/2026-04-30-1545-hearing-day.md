California law restricts insurance companies from using anti-SLAPP motions (CCP § 425.16) to avoid bad faith litigation. Specifically, CCP § 425.17 exempts lawsuits arising from commercial speech—such as insurance claims handling—from anti-SLAPP, ensuring insurers cannot easily dismiss lawsuits based on their business conduct.Key details regarding insurance and anti-SLAPP in California:Commercial Exception (§ 425.17): This statute prevents businesses (including insurers) from using anti-SLAPP to strike lawsuits against them when the complaint arises from commercial speech or actions, such as handling a claim, selling a policy, or providing insurance services.Bad Faith Claims: Courts generally hold that typical insurance bad faith actions are not "public interest" speech protected by anti-SLAPP, as they are private disputes over contractual duties rather than public debates.Ninth Circuit Precedent: Federal courts, interpreting California law, have confirmed that insurers cannot use anti-SLAPP motions to stop policyholders from filing bad faith claims related to the handling of lawsuits.Purpose: These restrictions protect against the abuse of anti-SLAPP laws, which were intended to protect public participation, not shield corporations from liability for commercial actions.If an insurance company is acting in a purely commercial capacity (e.g., investigating a claim), an anti-SLAPP motion is likely inapplicable under § 425.17.
