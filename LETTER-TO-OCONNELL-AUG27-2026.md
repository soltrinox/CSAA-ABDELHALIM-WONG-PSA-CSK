Tyler,

Your continued insistence on pushing the CCP § 391 vexatious litigant motion and CCP § 425.16(c) fee applications in the trial court ignores fundamental procedure. Under CCP § 916, our Notice of Appeal shifts review of the anti-SLAPP order to the Court of Appeal and automatically stays trial court proceedings on that order and its dependent rulings. Your § 391.3 motion relies entirely on that appealed strike order as its premise; thus, it cannot move forward. The court recognized this on August 26 by taking it off calendar, noting the stay, and ordering jurisdictional briefing. Your reliance on Pittman misses the mark entirely, as a voluntary dismissal bears no legal equivalent to a pending appeal.

The same statutory bar dooms your demand for "entire case" fees. An unfinalized strike order cannot anchor fee awards or justify security demands mid-appeal. You have 30 days to draft six pages on jurisdiction, and I suggest using that time to master how appellate stays actually function.

I am not sure if I told you this story; perhaps I did in a prior email:

"This is the story of a fairly clever man who was tasked with collecting firewood for his job. He had to spend every day picking up sticks just so they could stay warm. One day, on his way to collect sticks, he walked past a field that he had never really noticed before, which had danger signs posted all around it. But there were thousands of sticks there, all easy to grab, nobody was watching, and nobody cared. So, he started going in there and grabbing the easy stuff, the low-hanging fruit, as they say. He thought he was very smart, having found the easy way and the shortcut, enjoying the easy pickings. Finally, on a warm summer day, he reached down with an armload of sticks, relishing in how he had really parked himself in the easy lane. Reaching down, he grabbed another stick, but this one seemed a little different, even though it looked like a stick. And then, when he moved the bundle of sticks he had collected to get a better look at what he had picked up, the last thing he could remember thinking was, 'Wow, that viper really looked like a stick.'"

Be careful what you pick up. When overconfidence leads to shortcuts, attorneys commit their clients to irreversible positions.
